<p style="color: #000000;"><img src="https://ids.si.edu/ids/iiif/NMAH-JN2017-01326-000001/full/full/0/default.jpg" alt="Blackleg Vaccine Kit" width="100%" height="100%" /></p>
<p>&ldquo;Collecting&rdquo; is conceived broadly as an interdisciplinary approach to historical and current practices of acquiring, holding, organizing, and using objects and information for the purposes of cultural heritage and knowledge production. As such it is as inclusive of data and digital phenomena as it is of specimens and artifacts. And while the purview of study will include amateur, individual, and private collecting as hobby, it will address more centrally the role that collecting within institutional settings (i.e. the &ldquo;G.L.A.M. sector: galleries, libraries, archives, and museums) has performed in selectively holding and preserving evidence of human experience.</p>


{%- include heading name="activity" -%}


<p>In the previous Lab Activity, you have learned how to browse the Wellcome Collection and about digital collections, this activity is to search and source three digital collections for the items as part of your project. These digital collections include the one at the Library of Congress, the Wellcome Collection, and the Smithsonian's Collections. After the demonstration of each site, you will work with your group, first, to set up the parameters (format, subject, and others, as seen in the categories of metadata). Next, you will exercise some searching strategies. Finally, with each individual's particular interests, each of you will choose 2 different items from each site (6 items in total) as the candidates to be include in your group project pertaining to the subject of our course.</p>
<h4>Parameters</h4>
<p><span style="color: #000000;"><span>Parameters are different types of the information are the basic categories of metadata associated with any collected item in a collection. For instance, when you are on the homepage of the <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.si.edu/collections" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Smithsonian's Collections (SC)</a>, let's put "vaccine" in the search bar, and then you'll see different types of information on the left side of the page that can be refined ("Filter Results"): Topic, Date, Resource Type, and so on. These are the places where you can set up your parameters for searching. Click on each, and see what is in them. Each site of digital collections has similar and different parameters. When going through each digital collection, discuss with your group members, what parameters you would set up and why.</span></span></p>

<pre><span style="color: #000000;"><span>"Wildcard"</span></span></pre>
<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;">In the information studies, a wildcard character is a kind of placeholder represented by a single character, such as an asterisk (*), which can be interpreted as any literal characters or an empty string. It is useful in search. <br /></span></p>
<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;"><span>On the homepage of the <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.si.edu/collections" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Smithsonian's Collections (SC)</a><a class="inline_disabled" title="Link" href="https://loc.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">, </a></span>you can put a keyword on the search bar on top of the page. If you search "<strong><span style="font-size: 14pt;">vaccin</span><span style="font-size: 18pt;">*</span></strong>", there are 22,960 items as "Collection Images" available online as you see in the search returns. And then you can refine/filter the result by adding other filters, for instance, choosing "Vaccines" in Topic, and then you narrow down to 148 items. You can choose more than one filter to refine your search. The results of these items could also a good parameter of subjects for you to work on.</span></p>
<pre style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;">Digital Content</span></pre>
<p><span style="color: #000000;">Here are some criteria pointing what type of media/image/digital content you are looking for. Your items have to satisfy all these following three criteria: <br /></span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="color: #000000;"><span>Reputable institutions: Here are the reputable institutions that hold relevant digital contents: <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.loc.gov/collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Library of Congress Digital Collections (LoC)</a>, <a class="inline_disabled external" href="https://wellcomecollection.org/collections" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wellcome Collection (WC)</a>, and <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.si.edu/collections" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Smithsonian's Collections (SC)</a>. </span></span></li>
<li><span style="color: #000000;"><span>Linked digital content (i.e. media/images and metadata) from the original source. For instance, when you look at a source from one of above site, it is usually the case that that particular institution has the right to have the authoritative scan of the original work. When you import the media by using image URL, which is stable and often permanent. In other words, you are able to acquire the digital content by linking with its URL and not just by downloading it directly into your device.</span></span></li>
<li><span style="color: #000000;"><span>IIIF</span></span>
<p>IIIF (International Image Interoperability Framework) is a standard that institutions can follow to present their digital materials in a way that is high quality and "interoperable" (i.e. the same image can be viewed in different digital environments). The principle of <em>interoperability</em> enables content to be sourced and used in combination with materials derived from other participating institutions around the world.&nbsp;</p>
<p>All of these institutions that provide IIIF content indicate it with a small IIIF icon that looks like this: &nbsp;<img style="max-width: 30px;" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e8/International_Image_Interoperability_Framework_logo.png" alt="IIIF (triple eye eff) logo, which stands for International Image Interoperability Framework" width="30" height="30" />. Clicking the icon will load the object in its own viewer. The links provided above serve as web addresses of "manifests" for each item. Do not be alarmed if you click such a link and find yourself staring at a screen of indecipherable code! (that code <em>is&nbsp;</em>the manifest, a list of items and related information).</p>
</li>
</ul>
