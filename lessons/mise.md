<p>Migration has played a significant role in shaping the United States, both historically and culturally. Since its inception, the United States has been a destination for immigrants seeking new opportunities, freedoms, and a better life. From the arrival of the first settlers to the present day, people from all over the world have come to the United States, contributing to its diversity and growth. While immigration has been a source of strength and vitality for the country, it has also been a contentious issue, with debates over who should be allowed to enter, how many, and under what conditions. Understanding the complex history and impact of migration in the United States is essential to comprehending the country's past and present.</p>

Thus, we will compare two concrete images from the collections, archives, and galleries covered so far. Rigorously, we will offer an analysis of the topics surrounding political murals, farm work, migration, Chicana feminism, changes to education system, etc.</p>

{%- include heading name = 'example'  -%}

<p>These two photographs, taken from the photographic archive of the Argentine Rodrigo Abd, exemplify the consequences of COVID-19 in Latin America. In the photograph on the left, a homeless person can be seen in front of a furniture store. Their pose is almost a reflection of Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres' painting, "The Grand Odalisque" (1814). Like the woman in the Turkish harem, the man looks fixedly at the observer. However, she is comfortably reclining on a couch, while he rests on the street. The emphasis on oriental features in the French painter's work contrasts with the homeless man in the photograph. In the photograph on the right, the faces of parishioners who have died from COVID-19 can be seen contrasted in an empty church, and a priest anointing the images with incense. In the background, Giandomenico Tiepolo's painting, titled "Venetian Stations of the Cross, 11th Station, Crucifixion of Jesus," can be seen. It is said that Giandomenico deliberately created a claustrophobic atmosphere, seeking to create an almost unbreathable sense of drama, even though the narrative takes place outdoors. To achieve this, he piled up figures in the foreground, including the essential characters for the representation of the drama, as well as some others that help to create that unsettling atmosphere that invites reflection on the events represented. It is also, therefore, an inverted reflection of the "unsettling" image of the empty church.</p>

{%- include heading name = 'concept'  -%}

<p>There is an approach to the study of art, whether it be literature, painting, photography or film, that focuses on the construction of planes and details, similar to what is required in a building project. This is called structuralist theory. It serves to analyze and establish certain spatial categories that relate to composition, scale, and perception.</p>
<p>According to Roland Barthes, every image or journalistic photograph is a message. In this particular case, a clear message is presented, a political action with a strong response from the police force as its denotative meaning, while its connotative reading shows the repression of an inherent right, the excess use of force and the disproportionality of police methods. The image is intended to generate a great impact on the public due to the rawness of the image, as a representation of a situation of violence.</p>
<p>In order to understand the general plane offered by the image, I propose that any significant aspect be identified, such as: subjects, objects, background, framing, textures, shapes, lights/shadows, colors, materials, etc.</p>
<p>Once the details are recognized, we can generate a narrative or decipher the "coded" message by the photograph.</p>
