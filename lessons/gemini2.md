<h3 class="" style="background-color: #0073a3; color: #ffffff; padding: 0.2em 0.6em; border-radius: 30px;"><span style="font-size: 18pt;">💡 </span>Concept</h3>
<p><img style="float: left; margin: 0 15px 15px 0; max-width: 300px; border: 2px solid #000000;" src="https://iiif.library.ucla.edu/ark%3A%2F21198%2Fzz002h2f77/full/800,/0/default.jpg" alt="A page from Bonnie Cashin's personal scrapbook" width="175" height="214" /> Archives are not neutral repositories of truth; they are constructed, both by the institutions that hold them and the individuals who leave them behind. When we study the Bonnie Cashin collection, we are met with a fascinating tension between hyper-visibility and deliberate silence.</p>
<p>Cashin was born to an Armenian immigrant mother (Eunice, born Ovsanna). However, coming of age and building a career in an era steeped in American xenophobia and pressure to assimilate, Cashin almost entirely obscured her ethnic heritage. The "Bonnie Cashin" brand was marketed as quintessentially, effortlessly "All-American." To achieve professional success and avoid marginalization, she carefully curated her public persona, leaving intentional gaps in her personal history.</p>
<p>When historians look at archives, they must learn to "read the silences"&mdash;to ask not only what is preserved, but what has been left out, altered, or anglicized, and <em>why</em>.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>

<h3 class="" style="background-color: #0073a3; color: #ffffff; padding: 0.2em 0.6em; border-radius: 30px;">🖥️ &nbsp;<span style="color: #ffffff;">Activity</span></h3>
<p>In this lab, we will examine biographical materials, press clippings, and personal scrapbooks from the collection. Your task is to look for the "Americanness" she projected. How does she frame her identity in the press? What stories does she tell about her origins, and what stories are missing?</p>
