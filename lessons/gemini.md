## {{page.title}}


{%- include heading name="concept" -%}


<p><img style="float: left; margin: 0 15px 15px 0; max-width: 300px; border: 2px solid #000000;" src="https://iiif.library.ucla.edu/iiif/2/ark%3A%2F21198%2Fzz0002z106/full/500,/0/default.jpg" alt="A sketch of a Cashin handbag featuring prominent hardware" width="175" height="214" /> Prior to the mid-20th century, women's high fashion was often characterized by decorative fragility. Handbags were small, delicate objects meant to be carried delicately. Bonnie Cashin radically disrupted this. Recognizing the rise of the modern, independent career woman, she began designing for women who were commuting, traveling, and carrying their own weight&mdash;literally and metaphorically.</p>
<p>As fashion historian Stephanie Lake points out, Cashin was inspired by the convertible roofs of sports cars and the durable hardware of dog leashes. She introduced heavy-duty industrial elements into women's fashion, most notably the brass toggle or "turnlock" that became the signature of her work for Coach. By giving women oversized pockets and cross-body straps that freed their hands, Cashin's designs weren't just aesthetic choices; they were material interventions that facilitated physical and social mobility.</p>

{%- include heading name="activity" -%}


<p>In this lab, you will analyze how clothing and accessories dictate movement. You will use the Mirador viewer below to explore examples of Cashin's designs from the UCLA archive. Focus on the <strong>functional components</strong> of the garments and accessories. Then, you will copy the manifest of an item of your choice, paste it into the Cropping Tool, and extract a specific functional detail to share with the class.</p>

{%- include heading name="steps" -%}


<details style="color: #000000;">
<summary><strong>Cropping a IIIF image</strong></summary>
<ol style="list-style-type: decimal;">
<li>Copy the manifest URL of the item you would like to crop from the Materials section above and paste it into the Cropping Tool.</li>
<li>Click the thumbnail in the left column to load in the <strong>IIIF Cropping Tool</strong> viewer.</li>
<li>Zoom and view.</li>
<li>To crop a detail from the image, click the white pencil icon, and highlight the desired rectangle by clicking and dragging a rectangle from corner to corner.</li>
<li>A thumbnail of your selected detail will appear in a slider at the bottom of the Cropping Tool. You can repeat to collect multiple details.</li>
<li>Click the Copy icon <img src="https://static.thenounproject.com/png/361082-200.png" alt="Copy icon" width="24" height="24" /> on the left side of the thumbnail.</li>
</ol>
</details>
