## {{page.title}}


{%- include heading name="concept" -%}

<p><img style="float: left; margin: 0 15px 15px 0; max-width: 300px; border: 2px solid #000000;" src="https://iiif.library.ucla.edu/iiif/2/ark%3A%2F21198%2Fzz0000layer1/full/500,/0/default.jpg" alt="A sketch showing a Bonnie Cashin layered outfit" width="175" height="214" /> Bonnie Cashin is widely credited as a pioneer of "layering" in American fashion. Before Cashin, a woman's outfit was typically conceived as a single, rigid silhouette. Cashin, however, designed with a philosophy she called "dressing to live." She recognized that modern women were constantly moving between different environments&mdash;from brisk outdoor commutes to centrally heated offices, or traveling across different climate zones on airplanes.</p>
<p>To accommodate this, Cashin created modular components: thin knit turtlenecks, sturdy leather vests, canvas tunics, and voluminous "Noh" coats. These garments were designed to be mixed, matched, and literally peeled off as the temperature or activity dictated. This approach was highly textural, often mixing contrasting materials like soft cashmere with heavy suede. Layering wasn't just a styling trick; it was a deeply functional approach to modern living.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>

{%- include heading name="activity" -%}

<p>In this lab, you will analyze Cashin's approach to the modular wardrobe. Use the Mirador viewer below to explore examples of her layered ensembles or sketches showing multiple garments worn together. Focus on how the pieces interact: look at the contrasting textures, the necklines, and the voluminous shapes that allow garments to fit over one another. You will copy the manifest of an item, paste it into the Cropping Tool, and extract a specific detail that illustrates this layering concept.</p>

<ol style="list-style-type: decimal;">
