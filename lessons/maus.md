## {{page.title}}

{%- include heading name="concept" -%}

<p><img src="https://iiif.archive.org/image/iiif/3/the-complete-maus-2003%2FThe%20Complete%20Maus%20(2003)_jp2.zip%2FThe%20Complete%20Maus%20(2003)_jp2%2FThe%20Complete%20Maus%20(2003)_0208.jp2/63,125,1184,726/1200,/0/default.jpg" alt="thumbnail image" data-manifest="https://iiif.archivelab.org/iiif/the-complete-maus-2003/manifest.json" /></p>
<p>In "The Generation of Postmemory" Marianne Hirsch introduces a key concept for understanding historical records of political violence:</p>
<div class="page" title="Page 4">
<div class="layoutArea">
<div class="column">
<p style="padding-left: 40px;"><em><span style="font-family: georgia, palatino;">"Postmemory describes the relationship that the generation after those who witnessed cultural or collective trauma bears to the experiences of those who came before, experiences that they &ldquo;remember&rdquo; only by means of the stories, images, and behaviors among which they grew up. But these experiences&nbsp;were transmitted to them so deeply and actively as to seem to constitute memories in their own right. Postmemory&rsquo;s connection to the past is thus not actually mediated by recall but by imaginative investment, projection, and creation. To grow up with such overwhelming inherited memories, to be dominated by narratives that preceded one&rsquo;s birth or one&rsquo;s consciousness, is to risk having one&rsquo;s own stories and experiences displaced, even evacuated, by those of a previous generation. It is to be shaped, however indirectly, by traumatic events that still defy narrative reconstruction and exceed comprehension. These events happened in the past, but their effects continue into the present. This is, I believe, the experience of postmemory and the process of its generation."</span></em></p>
<p>Curiously, in addressing Art Spiegelman's&nbsp;<em>Maus,</em> a comic book account of the Holocaust as narrated by the artist's father, a survivor, Hirsch focuses first on photography, and not drawing (or graphic speech) as a medium of remembrance: "For me, it was the three <em>photographs</em> intercalated in Art Spiegelman&rsquo;s Maus that first elicited the need for a term that would describe the particular form of belated or inherited memory that I found in Spiegelman&rsquo;s work."</p>



{%- include heading name="activity" -%}


<p style="color: #000000;">For this hands-on lab activity, we will conceive of Spiegelman's&nbsp;<em>Maus&nbsp;</em>as an archive of visualized memories Spiegelman methodically collected and curated from his father Vladek, based upon Vladek's oral testimony. Additionally, following Hirsch, we will view&nbsp;<em>Maus&nbsp;</em>as<em>&nbsp;</em>a body of evidence about Art Spiegelman's own state of mind as a survivor of second-hand trauma. The focus of our study, however, will be upon his handiwork.</p>
<p><span style="color: #000000;">Like fingerprints at a crime scene, hand-drawn lines and words are uniquely expressive of their creator. Visualized scenery inside a frame reflects the perspective of the artist as much as the witness. What isn't shown is left to the reading viewer to fill in. Using concepts introduced by Scott McCloud, such as <a title="11. Toe the Line" href="https://bruinlearn.ucla.edu/courses/176658/pages/11-toe-the-line" data-course-type="wikiPages" data-published="true" data-api-endpoint="https://bruinlearn.ucla.edu/api/v1/courses/176658/pages/11-toe-the-line" data-api-returntype="Page">line-drawn cartoon</a>, the connecting <a title="5. Understanding Comics" href="https://bruinlearn.ucla.edu/courses/176658/pages/5-understanding-comics" data-course-type="wikiPages" data-published="true" data-api-endpoint="https://bruinlearn.ucla.edu/api/v1/courses/176658/pages/5-understanding-comics" data-api-returntype="Page">gutter</a> b</span><span style="color: #000000;">etween panels,&nbsp;and a simple cropping tool (embedded below) we will select, describe and interpret examples from </span><em style="color: #000000;">Maus.</em><span style="color: #000000;"> This exercise in a discussion-based setting will help prepare you collaboratively for your solo illustrated paper assignment that follows.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><i style="color: #000000;"><strong>NOTE: </strong>You are&nbsp;</i><span style="color: #000000;"><span><i>welcome</i></span></span><i style="color: #000000;">&nbsp;to use materials and ideas you submitted for this lab activity in Paper Assignment #3!</i></p>

<h4 style="text-align: center;"><strong>Description</strong></h4>
<p><span style="font-size: 12pt; font-family: georgia, palatino;">One sees the outside of the crematorium, with arrows identifying aboveground spaces (ovens) and belowground spaces (undressing rooms, gas chambers), along with a smaller diagram of the complete interior building--Crematorium II--lying vertically on the right across two views of the exterior. The diagram of the interior, a blueprint, shows the relation and scale of spaces like the toilet, coal storage, room for melting gold fillings, and 'corpse lift,' in addition to the incineration room and gas chamber. On the page's bottom tier, the panel depicts the ramp down to the so-called disinfection room from the perspective of someone walking down it. The final panel takes readers into the space of the undressing room with its signs designed to perpetuate the illusion of return: 'Important-remember your hook number.'</span></p>
<h4 style="text-align: center;"><strong>Observation &amp; Interpretation</strong></h4>
<p><span style="font-family: georgia, palatino;">On this page the crematorium chimney itself breaks the frame of reference, in its comics expression as in life: its stack bursts upward, jutting out of Spiegelman's topmost border, sitting conspicuously and ominously against white space. On the previous page an Auschwitz chimney also breaks the top of the frame, its wafting smoke joining Spiegelman's cigarette smoke in the above tier; even as a second-generation witness, the book implies, Art breathes Auschwitz, perhaps as a habit and perhaps to his detriment).</span></p>

In your brief (approx. 20-30 words) follow-up post, engage with the shared image directly, as well as the description, observations, and interpretations of details:</p>
<ul>
<li>as a viewer of your peer's post, do you think their cropped illustration serves effectively as&nbsp;<strong><em>evidence</em></strong> of the concepts and ideas they hope to illustrate?</li>
<li>as a reader, do you find their observations and interpretations <em><strong>plausible</strong></em>? (Whether or not you agree with them is not important; but whether you think they <em>could be</em> both informative and valid to a reasonable audience will help them refine their analyses)</li>
</ul>
