<p>"Ekphrasis," comes from the Greek word &epsilon;&kappa;&phi;&rho;&#940;&sigma;&epsilon;&iota;&sigmaf;, which is a literary or rhetorical description about &nbsp;a visual artistic work, like "Ode on a Greek Urn", by John Keats:

<a href="https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/44477/ode-on-a-grecian-urn" target="_blank">https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/44477/ode-on-a-grecian-urn</a>

</p>
<p>
Generally it can be applied to any activity that "translates" visual information into verbal.
</p>
<p>
Even though there are schools of criticism that emphasize a seemingly rivarly between visual and written arts, there are artists who prefer to cross the border/ the limit that separates them. We are going to be using "cross pollination", questioning the categories of "drawing" and "writing".
</p>

{%- include heading name="activity" -%}

<p>
Here we are going to use the practice of Ekphrasis in a relatively straightforward way, as an academic practice fundamental for visual description. Think about it as 
reading an image/photograph.
</p>
<p>
It can be an interesting and insightful process that requires careful observation and analysis. Here are some instructions that can help you:
</p>
<ol>
<li>
Take a moment to look at the image/photograph and identify what it is showing
. Is it a portrait, landscape, or a still life? What are the main subjects in the photograph? What is the setting?<br>

</li>
<li>
Analyze the composition.
&nbsp;Look at the placement of the subjects and how they are framed within the image. Pay attention to the use of lines, shapes, patterns.<br>

</li>
<li>
Consider the lighting. 
Is it natural or artificial? How does it affect the mood and tone of the photograph? Does it create any shadows or highlights that add to the composition?<br>

</li>
<li>
Consider the context
. What was happening at the time the photograph was taken? Who took the photograph, and why? What message or story is the photograph trying to convey?<br>

</li>
<li>
Pay attention to the details.
&nbsp;Are there any subtle elements that you might have missed at first glance? What do these details add to the overall composition and meaning of the photograph?<br>

</li>
<li>
Finally, think about your own interpretation
. What emotions or thoughts does it evoke for you? How does it make you feel, and why?<br>

</li>
</ol>
<p>
Remember: reading a photograph is a subjective process, and everyone may interpret it differently. These instructions can help guide you through the process, but ultimately, it is up to you to make your own meaning.
</p>

<h4>IIIF Image Cropping Tool</h4>
<p>Once you have selected a IIIF image. You can use the tool below. The cropping process is somewhat technical, but it can be easily mastered with patience following these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>
copy the first IIIF URL or find the IIIF icon (right click on it and choose "
Copy Link address"
) from the list of Resources above , and paste it into the bar "Enter the URL or a IIIF manifest" and click 
Submit
</li>
<li>
Take a few minutes to look at the photo and take in its details. I encourage you to think about what it might be trying to convey, and how it makes you feel.
</li>
<li>
click on the 
pencil icon
&nbsp;to select a detail-fragment of the image (adjust the corners of the cropping box until it isolates the detail). Notice that the box is "size-sensitive", so please 
choose a medium-to large size fragment
&nbsp;so the image is clear in your reply. 
</li>
<li>
once you are satisfied with the positioning and size of the detail, look for the automatically 
generated HTML
&nbsp;of your detail in the gray box. Select it all and copy it.
</li>
<li>
Finally, click on "reply" below, 
click on the &lt;/&gt; button
&nbsp;and 
paste the HTML code
. Then, write a 80-100 word description of what you see, and it does not need to be formal, or comprehensive. 
You can use the IIIF images I have chosen for you or you can look for your own in <a href="https://digital.library.ucla.edu/">https://digital.library.ucla.edu/</a>, as long as you embed a IIIF image (detail) with your written response. &nbsp;</li>
<li>Resize it as needed.</li>
</ol>
