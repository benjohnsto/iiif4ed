## {{page.title}}

{%- include heading name="concept" -%}

<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">As you have come to learn, IIIF is a set of standards for making high-resolution media and associated metadata available in an interoperable way. This does not mean, however, that all institutions choose to standardize the way they make their resources available.&nbsp; Individual institutions are free to design their repositories however they choose.&nbsp; Some institutions display the IIIF logo prominently within their collection websites.&nbsp; Other institutions might not use the logo at all, but instead provide links that read 'IIIF Manifest' or 'View in Mirador' or 'Open in Viewer'. This all can be a little confusing. Take for example the two IIIF-enabled repositories below, Princeton University Library and Brown University Library.</span></p>

<h3>Don't worry!</h3>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">You don't need to understand all that code. This file is called a <strong>manifest</strong>. A manifest is a file that encapsulates information about a digital object, such as metadata (title, artist, holding institution, etc) and one or many depictions of the object. Depictions are most often images, but can also be videos, audio files, or even 3d renderings.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Again, it is not important to understand the contents of this file. It is important to be able to <strong>USE IT</strong>! To use it, all you need is the URL (universal resource locator), a fancy term for web address. It is this web address that you will paste into various tools to make use of the digital object represented by the manifest.</span></p>



{%- include heading name="tools" -%}


<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">IIIF is interoperable.&nbsp; This means, among other things, that IIIF manifests can be opened in a variety of IIIF-enabled tools. IIIF-enabled tools are built to understand the IIIF standards.&nbsp; There are many types of tools, including those for simply viewing IIIF materials, but also for cropping and annotating.&nbsp; Two of the most common tools for viewing IIIF resources are <strong>Universal Viewer</strong> and <strong>Mirador</strong>.</span></p>
<h3><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://universalviewer.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img style="float: right;" src="logo.png" alt="Universal Viewer Logo" width="150" height="180" data-api-endpoint="https://canvas.instructure.com/api/v1/courses/10565702/files/276345484" data-api-returntype="File" /></a></h3>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Open the <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://universalviewer.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">link to the Universal Viewer</a>.&nbsp; Scroll down the resulting page to the section labeled <strong>View a IIIF Manifest</strong>.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Paste your URL into the box and click <strong>VIEW</strong>.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">You should now see a viewer with your object loaded into it. Most likely, you have seen this viewer before. It is very commonly used on Library and Museum websites.</span></p>

<h3>Mirador</h3>
<p><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://mcgrawcenter.github.io/mirador/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img style="float: right;" src="mirador-logo.svg" alt="mirador-logo.svg" width="167" height="162" data-api-endpoint="https://canvas.instructure.com/api/v1/courses/10565702/files/276591630" data-api-returntype="File" /></a><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Another common IIIF Viewer is <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://mcgrawcenter.github.io/mirador/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Mirador</a>. While Universal Viewer focuses on displaying a single object/manifest, Mirador is suited to viewing multiple objects side-by-side.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Click the blue, <strong>Start Here</strong> button on the left-hand side of the viewer, Click Add Resource at the bottom of the screen and then paste your manifest URL into the box.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Click Add to add your manifest to the list of resources.</span></p>
