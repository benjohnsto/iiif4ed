<h3 class="" style="background-color: #2774ae; color: #ffffff; border-color: #2774ae;"><i class="far fa-lightbulb" style="background-color: #ffd100; color: #000000;" aria-hidden="true"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>&nbsp; Concept</h3>
<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Annotation</strong> is a comment and/or other piece of information that is associated with a portion of a target object, such as a line of text in a document, a detail in an image, or a clip of video. Most people think of annotations as text, such as a label, tag, or comment. Annotations can also be, or include, media, such as an image.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;">An annotation can be used to add valuable information to a piece of media that is&nbsp;<em>about&nbsp;</em>the media itself. In that way it is somewhat different from metadata, which is information about the item, such as who made it, when, and where. Such annotations might simply identify something, a person or object, within an image. Annotations can also be used by an author for close observation and interpretation of a detail. In this scenario, annotation is used analytically, or&nbsp;<em>inductively</em>,&nbsp;as an incremental step toward a thesis or argument. In other words, annotations can serve an important role in making your media work for you as evidence, rather than simple illustration (or even decoration) to embellish text.&nbsp;</span></p>
    </div>
    <div id="kl_custom_block_3" class="">
<h3 class="" style="background-color: #2774ae; color: #ffffff; border-color: #2774ae;"><i class="" style="background-color: #ffd100; color: #000000;" aria-hidden="true"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>&nbsp; Review</h3>
<p>The hands-on activity that you just did demonstrate your developing proficiency of close and comparative viewing of visual content, through the practice of annotation, a key competency in digital scholarship. This kind of annotation has three basic elements:</p>
<ul style="color: #000000;">
<li><span style="color: #000000;">A title</span></li>
<li><span style="color: #000000;">A description or observation</span></li>
<li><span style="color: #000000;">An interpretive statement or an inference.<br /></span></li>
</ul>
<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Example:</strong> "Simple collar. The father's collar is mid-sized, white, and has just a bit of lace frill around the edges. It appears to express simple values or Puritanism."</span></p>
