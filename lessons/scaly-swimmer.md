<details style="color: #000000;">
    <summary><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Scalar</strong><br />So what, exactly, is a Scalar?&nbsp;&nbsp;</span></summary>
    <p>In mathematics, a<em> scalar</em> is one of two or more values that define a <em>vector.</em> Vectors can be used to understand worldly problems, such as how to cross the Tollan atl river. To reach a point directly opposite on the far side and not get carried away and drown, a swimmer must aim for a point upriver (v). Thus the actual distance swum is greater than the distance across (y), and the actual direction is at a diagonal, once one accounts for the current of the river . &nbsp;</p>
    <img style="display: block; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;" role="presentation" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/87/Vector_components.svg" alt="" />
    <p>&nbsp;</p>
    <p>In the early modern history of the Digital Humanities (about a decade ago), Scalar was launched as a digital&nbsp;authoring tool to expand capacity for scholarly multimedia publication by the design and editorial team of <em>Vectors</em>, an online digital scholarly journal. The tool outlived the journal, which once featured a playful interactive scribbling device for drawing one's own pathway through content, before the underlying web technology became obsolete (<a class="inline_disabled" href="http://vectors.usc.edu/issues/index.php?issue=7" target="_blank" rel="noopener">here is what's left of it</a>).&nbsp;</p>
    <p>The mathematical concepts that inspired the names of these digital environments may seem simple in comparison with the big ideas they exemplified: the "<a class="inline_disabled" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web" target="_blank" rel="noopener">semantic web</a>," for example, which is only now beginning to realize its early vision.&nbsp;</p>
    <img style="display: block; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;" src="https://i.ytimg.com/vi/T6k4IpSOgHY/mqdefault.jpg" alt="Scalar logo" width="320" height="180" />
    <p>The concept we will use as a starting point here for understanding Scalar relates in a way to the mathematical scalar as a value that is both important in itself (a scalar), and important to the extent it intersects with other values (a vector). For example:</p>
    <ul>
        <li>An image is important in itself; in relation to another image, or set of images, it takes on a new significance</li>
        <li>Text provides information about a topic; media represent subjects; in combination, media and text comprise a synthetic product.</li>
    </ul>
    <p><span style="font-family: inherit; font-size: 1rem;">Scalar is an online scholarly authoring platform designed on the concept of a book. It allows you to present media alongside text as exhibits for readers to see for themselves. And it helps you use exhibits as evidence in support of your argument or thesis through comparative analysis and annotation.&nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp;</span>We could approach Scalar the easy way and say: it's a website. We are going to approach it from the other side and say: it's a relational data structure that will help us think about the history of Tenochtitlan and the Florentine Codex in interesting ways, if we don't drown.&nbsp;</p>
    <p><span>Look for yourself at the "About Scalar" page on the ANVC website, look at the graphics, and think about the bold proposition that "anything can do anything to anything." What do you think that means?</span></p>
