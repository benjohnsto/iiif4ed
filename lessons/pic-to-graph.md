{%- include heading name="concept" -%}


<p><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/book/11/folio/225v?spTexts=&amp;nhTexts=" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img src="https://media.getty.edu/iiif/image/90e86ebb-efee-4abb-8d2f-a781cb02682f/1467,5097,2023,675/1200,/0/default.jpg" alt="Pictographic image of a human hand hovering over water from Florentine Codex, Book 11" width="100%" height="100%" /></a></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><strong>Code</strong> is a string of characters that signify something according to specific rules and conventions. The meaning of the symbols might be very different from how they appear at first glance. They can also have relational or "syntactical" significance: what they mean can depend on how they are positioned relative to each other. This is as true for historic indigenous pictographic images as it is for 21st-Century digital technologies</span></p>


{%- include heading name="activity" -%}


<p><span style="font-size: 12pt;">For our first hands-on "digitl" Lab Activity, we will jump into the deep end. Using HTML code, which is symbolized by the three characters &lt;/&gt;, we will literally <em>bring into being</em> a thing to view and play with. Following Steps, below, you will each solve a puzzle and share your impressions of it.</span></p>

<details style="color: #000000;">
            <summary><span style="color: #000000; font-size: 12pt;"><strong>Digitl Slide Activity</strong><br />Read the following steps through, and then proceed one at a time through the activity. <span style="color: #236fa1;"><em>(Click to open and close)</em></span></span></summary>
            <ol>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Take a look at the characters in the gray box above. Squint a little, then look closely, and see what patterns emerge at different levels of "zoom,' such as repetition, symmetry, formulas.&nbsp;</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Highlight and copy the entire block of code.</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Scroll to the bottom of the screen, and click <strong>Reply</strong></span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">When the "WYSIWYG" (<span style="text-decoration: underline;">w</span>hat <span style="text-decoration: underline;">y</span>ou <span style="text-decoration: underline;">s</span>ee <span style="text-decoration: underline;">i</span>s <span style="text-decoration: underline;">w</span>hat <span style="text-decoration: underline;">y</span>ou <span style="text-decoration: underline;">g</span>et) editor appears, look just under the box to the right for the HTML symbol <strong>&lt;/&gt;</strong> and click it. You should see a gray field and the number 1.</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Paste your code into the field.</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Click the HTML symbol &lt;/&gt; again to switch back to the default editor. You should see something you can play with.</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">To the right of the HTML &lt;/&gt; symbol is an eight-dot "resize" symbol <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Braille8_Dots-14253678.svg/154px-Braille8_Dots-14253678.svg.png" alt="8 dot computer symbol" width="21" height="37" />, which you can slide down to enlarge your WYSIWYG editing field. Use it to view the full image set at once (you may need to do this a couple times).</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Following standard rules of a <a class="inline_disabled" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliding_puzzle" target="_blank" rel="noopener">sliding puzzle</a>, your task is to unscramble an image: <strong>click into any image and drag or cut and paste it into the only empty box, until you have "solved" the puzzle by putting all its scrambled pieces in the right order</strong>.</span></li>
                <li><span style="font-size: 12pt;">After you've put everything in its place, click the HTML &lt;/&gt; symbol one more time, and look at the code you re-produced. What similarities and/or differences do you see from the sequence you started with, if any?</span></li>
            </ol>
