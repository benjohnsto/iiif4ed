<h4><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Framing the Assignment</span></h4>
<p style="padding-left: 440px;"><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>Blood and destruction shall be so in use,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>And dreadful objects so familiar,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>That mothers shall but smile when they behold</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>Their infants quartered with the hands of war,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>All pity choked with custom of fell deeds,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>And Caesar&rsquo;s spirit, ranging for revenge,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>With Ate by his side come hot from hell,</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>Shall in these confines with a monarch&rsquo;s voice</em></span><br /><span style="font-size: 12pt;"><em>Cry &ldquo;Havoc!&rdquo; and let slip the dogs of war..</em></span></p>
<p style="padding-left: 520px;"><span style="font-size: 12pt;">-Shakespeare,<em> Julius Caesar</em></span></p>
<p style="color: #000000;">When Stephen College was hanged, drawn and quartered for treason in 1682 none was giddier about it than Roger L'Estrange. The two had been at each other's throats for years, and had "Towzer" L'Estrange not slipped the noose years earlier, College would have been no less delighted. The bloodthirstiness of political discourse is evident across the spectrum of primary source published documents from the period, from pamphlet to play, from broadside to ballad, and playing cards to poem. Even humor, "children's" literature (e.g. fables), and art were weaponized in the religious-political battles to the death between what emerged as our two party (Whig v. Tory) system.</p>
<p style="color: #000000;">Discourse of all kinds became entangled with violent religious symbolism, public pageantry, and collective historical traumas, such as the Great Fire of London (1666), and words and images were rich with allusion and allegory. Put more simply: documents said more than was evident at first glance. Historians are still hard at work decoding and contextualizing materials that have been available for over 300 years, and now it's time for us to have a crack at it. The Clark Library holds a treasure trove of materials from the late Restoration period, which we will soon view with our own eyes. To prepare us for that opportunity, we have one more summative activity to develop your methodological proficiencies as undergraduate historians: a thesis-driven analysis of one primary source document.&nbsp;</p>
<p style="color: #000000;">Are a College type or a more of a L'Estranger? Take your pick. Whatever side of the hellish Whig/Tory divide you find yourself on, we have an item for you.&nbsp;</p>
<h4 style="color: #000000;">Scaffolding Information Literacy</h4>
<p><span style="color: #000000;">First, however, a brief word from our sponsor, the Association for Colledge &amp; Research Libraries (ACRL). You already had a stab at </span><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework#exploration" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><i style="color: #000000;"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i><i style="color: #000000;"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i></a><span style="color: #000000;"><span><i><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework#exploration" target="_blank" rel="noopener">searching as strategic exploration</a>.&nbsp;</i></span></span><span style="color: #000000;">Another scaffold, or "</span><em style="color: #000000;">Frame"</em><span style="color: #000000;"> of information literacy from which to hang your inquiry is:</span></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong style="color: #000000;">RESEARCH AS INQUIRY</strong></p>
<p style="color: #000000;">We quote from <strong><a class="inline_disabled" href="https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework#inquiry" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the original document</a>:</strong></p>
<p style="padding-left: 40px;"><strong>Research is iterative and depends upon asking increasingly complex or new questions whose answers in turn develop additional questions or lines of inquiry in any field.</strong></p>
<p style="padding-left: 40px;">Experts see inquiry as a process that focuses on problems or questions in a discipline or between disciplines that are open or unresolved. Experts recognize the collaborative effort within a discipline to extend the knowledge in that field. Many times, this process includes points of disagreement where debate and dialogue work to deepen the conversations around knowledge. This process of inquiry extends beyond the academic world to the community at large, and the process of inquiry may focus upon personal, professional, or societal needs. The spectrum of inquiry ranges from asking simple questions that depend upon basic recapitulation of knowledge to increasingly sophisticated abilities to refine research questions, use more advanced research methods, and explore more diverse disciplinary perspectives. Novice learners acquire strategic perspectives on inquiry and a greater repertoire of investigative methods.</p>

<p><span data-contrast="none">The analysis and interpretation of primary sources is the most important task of the inquiring historian. In this assignment, you will demonstrate proficiencies that you have learned in Unit 1 by penning a 3-4 page, doubled-spaced document in which you offer a close reading and analysis of one such source. The paper will be <strong>due on Thursday, October 13</strong>, to be turned in on our BruinLearn site. There will be two sources from which you will <strong>choose </strong></span><strong>one</strong><span data-contrast="none"> to write about.&nbsp;&nbsp;</span><span data-ccp-props="{}">&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span data-contrast="none">The purpose of your paper is to present an argument for the most important thing that the source that you select reveals about the subject and period under study. The sources are provided below, with additional linked <em>tertiary </em>(encyclopedic<em>)&nbsp;</em>information about the authors for context and reference.&nbsp;You can, of course, consult outside reference sources, such as the&nbsp;</span><i><span data-contrast="none">Oxford English Dictionary</span></i> (OED)<span data-contrast="none">&nbsp;to look up unfamiliar words, and consider other primary and secondary sources that have been assigned and/or discussed in class. </span></p>
<p><span data-contrast="none"><em><strong>Alert: this</strong></em><strong style="font-style: italic;"> is&nbsp;</strong></span><span data-ccp-props="{}"><strong><em>not a research paper.</em>&nbsp;</strong>It is a focused primary source analysis of a single item from your perspective.&nbsp;</span><span data-contrast="none">As you begin the assignment, bear in mind that each historian brings a unique perspective to the analysis of primary sources, which can result in different interpretations of any single document. Therefore, there is no single &ldquo;correct&rdquo; interpretation of any source. Your job is to persuade your reader of </span><i><span data-contrast="none">your</span></i><span data-contrast="none"> point of view, based on your careful reading and analysis.&nbsp;&nbsp;</span><span data-ccp-props="{}">While you are free to discuss this assignment with your colleagues, the paper you turn in must consist entirely of your own work. The paper will be evaluated for specificity of observations and interpretations, and clarity, content and effectiveness of your argument. A clear style and basic writing skills are also essential.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</span></p>
