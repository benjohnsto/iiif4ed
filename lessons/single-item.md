## {{page.title}}

<p class='callout'>
Students explore a single work, such as a book, a scroll, or other detailed work looking for details that interest them or that might suggest further research. Presented with a manifest loaded in Mirador, students look for details they would like to discuss in class or that they would like to research further. The students’ ideas and contributions are submitted to a discussion board.
</p>

<h2>Unit 2 Lab Activity 1: Exquisite Crops </h2>

His head and body are made up of various plants" width="100%" height="100%" /><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Giuseppe Arcimboldo's "Vertumnus" (1591), a fecund portrait of Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II, asks us to reconsider the matter of a whole in relation to its parts, a provocation as much about scientific inquiry as it was about 16th century empire.</span></p>

{%- include activity.html -%}

<p style="color: #000000;"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">This activity is inspired by a much later Surrealist game for co-authoring a poem or picture called <em>cadavre exquis</em> or "exquisite corpse." The rules and materials of exquisite corpse vary, but in most versions a sheet of paper is folded to conceal the whole, and participants take turns filling in successive sections of drawing or writing, based only upon the last lines from the section before. Our adaptation, which we will call "exquisite <em>crops</em>," uses the cropping tool to excerpt portions of images of plants (as well as people, animals, gods, or anything else).</span></p>
