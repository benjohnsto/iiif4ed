<p><img style="float: right; padding: 0px 0px 10px 10px;" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/David_et_Goliath_-_Daniele_da_Volterra_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_566_-_verso_-_cadre.jpg/440px-David_et_Goliath_-_Daniele_da_Volterra_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_566_-_verso_-_cadre.jpg" alt="David and Goliath" width="25%" height="25%" /></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 18pt;">The blood sport of primacy in the arts between 1D, 2D, and 3D mediums was contested half a millennium ago by mail. In 1546, at the peak of the Renaissance, poet Benedetto Varchi (1502-1565) sent out a questionnaire to his multitalented peers asking them to rank literature, painting, and sculpture. The epistolary exchange, known as the <em>Paragone</em> ("comparison") debates, yielded foundational insights into artistic representation by the period's lauded practitioners. <img style="float: left; padding: 0px 10px 10px 0px;" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/Laocoon_Pio-Clementino_Inv1059-1064-1067.jpg" alt="Laocoon bent" width="25%" height="25%" />Michelangelo, for one, feigned neutrality, but his muscular statuary, inspired by the recently unearthed Laoco&ouml;n, loomed menacingly and challenged all comers. Mannerist aesthetics in the round twisted binary paradigms, such as <em>contrapposto, </em>into suffocating spirals of <em>figura</em> <em>serpentinata</em>. Daniele de la Volterra counterpunched with a double-sided canvas of David atop Goliath. Though Volterra's flashy entrant struck a low blow, in the ensuing melee, most materialists concede that screens won the day. Ask an educator: will a student discriminate photograph from painting, drawing, illustration, or illumination? Pervasive media technologies render the world once again flat: a digital facsimile of an ephemeral object is just another image. &nbsp;&nbsp;</span></p>


{%- include heading name="activity" -%}

<p><span style="font-size: 18pt;">To resist and not succumb to supremacy of images (especially in archival high resolution), students can "authenticate" visual information by drawing direct perceptual associations between digital surrogates and the physical artifacts they represent. For this lab activity we will view 360 video recordings of student physical encounters with patent medicine trade cards as ephemeral objects of inquiry, and correlate them with our own "hands-on" experience with the same objects in IIIF-compatible interactive viewers.</span></p>
